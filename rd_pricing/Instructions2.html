Your earnings in this part will be measured in points. These points will be converted to U.S. dollars at the rate of 1500 points = $1.

In Part 1, you will take on the role of a producer who sells a product to computerized buyers. Participants will be divided into industries. Each industry consists of two producers who produce and sell similar products.

You will represent one producer in an industry. The other producer in your industry is another participant in this room, whom we will refer to as your counterpart. Your counterpart faces the same conditions as you. You will never learn your counterpart's identity.

Part 1 consists of a sequence of interactions, which we call matches. At the beginning of each match, you will be randomly paired with another participant.

Each match consists of two stages:

  1. Investment stage: You and your counterpart each make an investment decision. The investment decisions made at the beginning of the match apply to all pricing rounds in that match.
  2. Pricing stage: You and your counterpart each make a price choice in every pricing round. The number of pricing rounds in a match is randomly determined.

The customers who purchase your products are simulated by the computer. The higher the price of one producer's product relative to the price of the other producer's product, the fewer units the computerized buyers will purchase from the higher-priced producer and the more units they will purchase from the lower-priced producer.

Your earnings depend on your investment decision, your price choice, and the investment and price choices of your counterpart. Investment decisions affect production costs. Price choices affect the number of units sold and the earnings from those sales.

Your production costs and the buyers' demand together determine your earnings. This information will be presented in earnings tables, which will be explained in more detail later.

At the end of each pricing round, you will learn your counterpart's price choice and the number of points you earned in that round.

The number of pricing rounds in each match is randomly determined. After every pricing round, there is an 80% probability that the match will continue for another pricing round and a 20% probability that the match will end.

This continuation probability is the same after every pricing round. For example:

  • If you are in round 2, there is an 80% probability that there will be a third round.
  • If you are in round 9, there is also an 80% probability that there will be a tenth round.

At the end of each pricing round, the computer randomly draws a number from 1 to 100, with each number being equally likely.

  • If the number drawn is between 1 and 80, the match continues for another pricing round.
  • If the number drawn is between 81 and 100, the match ends.

If the match continues, the investment decisions made at the beginning of the match continue to apply. You and your counterpart will each make a new price choice for the next pricing round.

If the match ends, a new match begins. You will be randomly paired with another participant, and you and your new counterpart will each make a new investment decision before beginning the pricing stage of the new match.

The computer will randomly select one match for payment. You will be paid for all pricing rounds in the selected match. You will also receive a $5 show-up fee. The more points you earn, the more money you will receive at the end of the experiment.
